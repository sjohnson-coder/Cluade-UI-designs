What this study found

There is no loophole in XAUUSD's direction. Yesterday's return explains 0.018% of the variance in today's. Every classic Smart Money / ICT liquidity model tested here — twenty variants of sweep-and-reclaim across the London and New York killzones — came out at or below break-even before costs, and negative after them. News direction is a coin flip. The single largest statistical anomaly in the raw data turned out to be a stale price print.

There is a loophole in XAUUSD's clock. Volatility is roughly three thousand times more forecastable than direction, and gold's entire risk-adjusted return over 11.7 years was earned while Asia was awake and given back while New York traded. Re-weighting exposure by session — without predicting a single price — lifted the Sharpe ratio from 0.55 to 1.02 and cut the maximum drawdown from −28.3% to −18.3% on the same bars.

That is the whole finding. It is worth building. It is not a 100% win rate, and the last section explains why nothing ever will be.

Section 01 · The premise

A 100% win rate is a dial, not an edge

Before hunting for one, it is worth seeing exactly what a near-perfect win rate is made of — because it is trivially easy to manufacture and tells you nothing about whether a system makes money.

Take one setup from this study: fade a sweep of the Asian session high during the London killzone. Hold the entry rule, the stop and the sample completely fixed. Change only the profit target. Here is what happens across 1,107 real trades.

The win rate moves from 24% to 76% and the strategy stays exactly as unprofitable. Push the target down to 0.05 R and remove costs entirely — the most favourable case that can be constructed — and the same setup posts a 94.4% win rate: 1,045 wins against 62 losses, for a net of −9.7 R.

What a 95%-win-rate bot actually is

It is this table taken to its limit. Tiny targets, distant stops, and a run of wins long enough to look like proof. Grid and martingale expert advisors reach the same place by never realising a loss at all — they average into drawdown, so the win rate approaches 100% by construction and the losses accumulate off the books as open exposure. They work until a trend runs, and then they return the year in a week.

Any vendor quoting a win rate without an expectancy, a profit factor and a maximum drawdown is quoting the one number that can be set to anything.

So the target of this study is not win rate. It is expectancy that survives cost, an out-of-sample split, and a data-quality audit. Three things almost nothing survived.

Section 02 · Method

How the data was built, and the hour that lies

Two decisions determine whether an intraday gold study produces knowledge or noise. Both were wrong in the obvious version of this analysis.

The timezone almost everyone gets wrong

The source publishes its one-minute XAUUSD export as “Eastern Standard Time, no daylight savings”. It is not. Anchoring on two events whose wall-clock time is fixed by definition — Non-Farm Payrolls at 08:30 New York, and the London PM gold fix at 15:00 London — both land on the identical stamp in January and in July. They can only do that if the stamps already carry New York daylight time.

Taking the documentation at its word shifts every summer observation by one hour, which smears each session boundary across two hours for eight months of every year and quietly destroys any session-level result. Every figure in this report was computed after that check passed, and verify_timezone() re-runs it.

The largest anomaly in the data is not real

Scanning all 24 hours for drift, one hour dominates everything: 18:00 New York, the session reopen, at +2.87 bp mean with a 60.5% hit rate and t = 7.75. Over 11.7 years it accumulates $2,131 per ounce held. It looks like the loophole.

It is a stale quote. Decomposing that hour minute by minute, 42.8% of the entire effect comes from the single first print of the session, and 61.2% from the first five minutes. The reopen bar carries the last quote from before the daily break, so measuring open-to-close on it manufactures a return that no order could ever have filled. Every other hour of the day is unremarkable on this test — the artifact is isolated to the reopen.

Remove that one print and the effect collapses from t = 7.75 to t = 2.66, and a $0.30 spread turns it negative. This is not a quirk of one vendor: researchers hit the identical trap measuring overnight drift in the GDX gold-miners ETF, where an apparent 30%+ annualised anomaly largely evaporated once execution was tested on one-minute data.[6]

The rule this imposes on everything below

Any edge concentrated in the first or last print of a session, a day, or a data file is a data-quality artifact until proven otherwise. Test every candidate with the boundary bar excluded. This one check eliminated the strongest-looking result in the study.

Test discipline

  • Costs are always charged. $0.35/oz round trip as standard, with a full sensitivity sweep from $0.10 (futures) to $1.00 (wide retail CFD).
  • Bar-by-bar resolution. Stops and targets are walked forward on M1 so intrabar ordering is never assumed favourably.
  • Split sample. In-sample 2015–2021, out-of-sample 2022–2026. A sign flip between them is treated as failure, not as a regime story.
  • Causal signals only. Thresholds are computed from trailing data. One promising result — FOMC-day momentum — survived only while its detection threshold was fitted on the full sample, and died the moment it was made causal.
  • Returns, not dollars. Gold went from $1,184 to $4,572 across the sample. Dollar P&L would be dominated by the last two years, so everything is measured in basis points or ATR.
Section 03 · The negative results

What does not work

These are the strategies the retail world sells hardest. They were tested honestly, at scale, and reported here whatever the outcome — a negative result on 1,100 trades is more useful than a positive one on 30.

Smart Money Concepts: twenty variants, twenty failures

The core SMC/ICT mechanic is the liquidity grab: price sweeps a known pool of stops — the Asian session high or low, the previous day's high or low — then reverses back through the level, and you trade the failed break. It is a genuinely plausible mechanism and the levels are real. Every combination of reference level, killzone window and direction was tested at a 2 R target.

The diagnosis matters more than the verdict. Run the same trades at zero cost and the pattern is roughly break-even — the London Asian-high fade posts a profit factor of 1.06, the NY previous-day-high fade 1.11. The pattern is not random. It is simply too small to pay for itself.

The reason is mechanical. A reclaim entry sits close to the swept extreme, so the stop is tight: a median of $3.05, or 0.14 ATR. A $0.35 spread against a $3.05 stop is 11.5% of the risk on every single trade. No 50/50 pattern survives an 11.5% toll. Tightening the stop — which is what most SMC teaching material advises, in the name of "better R:R" — makes this strictly worse, because it raises the toll while leaving the edge alone.

This lines up with the independent literature. No peer-reviewed study establishes SMC/ICT profitability, and the patterns are defined loosely enough that two traders mark the same chart differently.[1] The favourable community backtests circulating — 61% win rates, profit factors above 2 — come from discretionary marking on small samples with no cost model, which is exactly the procedure this section was designed to replace.

Trading the news direction

The 08:30 New York release window is the most violent five minutes of the gold day. On Non-Farm Payroll Fridays it prints a median range of $8.23 against $1.07 in a quiet 07:00 window — 7.7× normal, and 35.1% of the entire day's range compressed into five minutes.

The volatility is completely reliable. The direction is not. Measure the first five-minute impulse and ask whether price is still on that side at the close: 50.4% on NFP days, 48.9% on other days. That is a coin flip, and a coin flip taken at the widest spread of the day is a losing proposition.

An apparent exception is instructive. Days with a large 14:00 spike — FOMC decisions and minutes — initially showed 57.7% continuation to the close, the strongest news result in the study. That threshold was set from a full-sample quantile. Rebuilt causally, from a trailing median that only uses data available at the time, the effect vanishes: t = −0.08, with in-sample and out-of-sample halves disagreeing in sign. It was look-ahead bias, not an edge.

Section 04 · The real structure

The clock is the loophole

Direction is not forecastable. Timing and magnitude are — overwhelmingly so. This is where the exploitable structure in XAUUSD actually lives.

Volatility, R²0.563yesterday's ATR predicting today's range
Direction, R²0.00018yesterday's return predicting today's
Ratio3,160×how much more forecastable volatility is
Range autocorrelation+0.80at 1-day lag; returns are +0.013

Every hour of the trading day is not equal, and the inequality is stable enough to build on. Three of the twenty-three hours carry over a fifth of the average day's entire range.

Gold's return is earned overnight and given back in New York

This is the central result of the study. Split every session into its three components and compound each one separately, holding gold during that window only, with the reopen print excluded.

Exhibit 7 · Risk and return by session exposure, 11.7 years
ExposureMarket hoursReturn p.a.VolatilitySharpeMax drawdown
Buy & hold (all sessions)237.69%15.6%0.55−28.3%

The 08:00–12:00 New York window spans 60.7% of the average daily range and delivers none of the return; the Asian session spans 48% of it and delivers all of it. The Asian drift measures +2.59 bp per session with t = 3.00 (p = 0.0027) and is positive in 10 of 12 calendar years; split into thirds it runs +2.74, +0.74 and +4.55 bp. The 2019–2022 stretch is weak enough to be honest about — this is a real effect, not a metronome.

It is also not unique to gold, which is a point in its favour. The overnight drift is one of the most durable anomalies in finance: equity index returns accrue almost entirely outside the cash session, and the New York Fed attributes the pattern to dealer inventory management — intermediaries absorb order imbalances into the close and are compensated for carrying that risk through the illiquid hours.[5] Gold has the same dealer structure and the same overnight handoff between Asian physical demand and Western futures positioning. A mechanism that already explains the effect elsewhere is a far better reason to trust it than a backtest alone.

Seasonality, and a volatility regime that changed everything

The day-of-week pattern is more robust and matches the published literature: Friday is the strongest day (+0.086% mean, 55.7% positive, t = 1.95), Monday the flattest at 50.5%. The weekly high forms on a Friday 27.4% of the time while Sunday and Monday together produce 33.7% of weekly lows — an early-week low, late-week high rhythm consistent with the Friday effect documented across Tokyo, London and New York gold markets.[3]

The single most important engineering consequence

Every threshold in a gold bot — stops, targets, breakout filters, trailing distances, position size — must be expressed as a multiple of ATR, never in dollars or pips. A $30 stop was 2.4 average daily ranges in 2018 and is 0.23 of one in 2026. The same code, untouched, silently mutated from a swing system into a scalper. This is the most common cause of a gold EA that "stopped working."

Section 05 · The construction

A bot that trades the clock, not the chart

The strategy that survives makes no directional forecast at all. It decides how much gold to hold as a function of the hour and of realised volatility — the two things that are actually forecastable.

Exhibit 10 · Session-weighted exposure vs. buy-and-hold, identical bars

Weights are Asia / London / New York. The 1.5 / 0.5 / 0.0 construction earns a comparable return to holding gold outright, at 72% of the volatility, with a drawdown 10 percentage points shallower and zero exposure during the most violent nine hours of the day. Sharpe close to doubles.

Layering a volatility target on top — scaling notional to hold realised volatility near a constant, capped at 1.5× — adds a little more: on buy-and-hold alone it lifts Sharpe from 0.78 to 0.80 and trims the worst day from −9.1% to −6.1%. It is a modest gain in return terms and a large one in survivability, which is the right trade for a leveraged instrument.

Cost is the entire question

The gross Asian drift is 2.59 bp per session, or about $0.57 per ounce at current prices. A typical retail XAUUSD spread is 2–4 bp of price. That is not a detail at the margin — it is the difference between the strategy and its opposite.

The practical conclusion

Build this on CME gold futures — GC, or MGC at 10 oz for smaller accounts — not on a retail CFD. One tick is $0.10/oz and commission adds $0.02–0.04. That is a third of the cost that kills the strategy. If a CFD is the only option, the venue must be an ECN with a documented sub-$0.30 average XAUUSD spread, verified on your own fills during the 18:00–02:00 window rather than from the broker's marketing page — spreads widen precisely at the rollover this strategy trades into.

The lower-turnover overlay in Exhibit 10 is the better answer to this problem: it adjusts weight rather than round-tripping the whole position every night, so the same edge carries far less cost.

Section 06 · Implementation

Building it

The reference implementation ships with this report: bot/session_overlay.py for the logic and bot/XAUUSDSessionOverlay.mq5 for MetaTrader 5.

The specification

  1. Clock, in New York time with DST. Asia 18:05–02:00 at weight 1.5, London 02:00–08:00 at 0.5, New York 08:00–17:00 at 0.0, flat through the 17:00–18:05 rollover. Entry at 18:05, never 18:00 — that five minutes is the artifact from Section 02, and it is also when the spread is at its widest.
  2. Volatility-targeted size. Notional = equity × session weight × min(target vol / realised 20d vol, 1.5). This is what keeps the bot alive across the regime shift in Exhibit 9.
  3. Spread gate. If the spread exceeds $0.35/oz, do not increase exposure. Reducing risk is always permitted. This single rule is what separates the viable rows of Exhibit 11 from the dead ones.
  4. Trend filter. Flat below the 200-day moving average. It costs return in the sample and cuts the tail — take it if you cannot sit through a 1980-style decade, skip it if you can.
  5. Flat before the weekend. Friday 15:00 New York. Weekend gap risk is uncompensated: the median Monday gap is $0.33 and 97% fill, so there is nothing to earn and a fat tail to carry.
  6. Hard per-session stop at 2% of equity, checked before any sizing logic runs.
Three implementation details that will silently break this

Broker server time is not New York time. Most MT5 brokers run at GMT+2/+3 and shift with European DST, not American. The EA converts through GMT and applies the US rule explicitly; its DST routine was verified against 105,192 hourly stamps from 2015–2026 with zero mismatches. Hard-coding a fixed offset puts every session boundary an hour out for part of the year.

Swap and financing. A held gold position pays or receives a nightly swap, and the Asian window straddles the rollover where it is charged. On many retail CFDs the short swap is punitive and the long swap is merely bad. Model it — a −$3/lot/night swap on a strategy earning $0.57/oz is not a rounding error. Futures avoid this entirely; the cost is in the basis instead.

Backtest on your own broker's ticks. The results here come from one aggregated feed. Your fills, spread profile and rollover behaviour differ, and this strategy lives inside a margin narrow enough that the difference matters.

What to build next, in order

  1. Verify the edge exists on your venue. Log the 18:05–02:00 return and your actual round-trip cost for 60 sessions before risking anything. If your cost exceeds $0.35/oz, stop — no amount of logic downstream recovers a negative gross edge.
  2. Paper-trade the overlay for a quarter. The expected number of sessions to distinguish this edge from zero is in the hundreds, so resist re-tuning on a bad month.
  3. Add the volatility forecast, not a price forecast. The 0.563 R² in Exhibit 4's companion statistics is where further modelling actually pays — better range forecasts improve sizing, and sizing is where this strategy's Sharpe comes from.
  4. Consider selling the New York volatility instead of avoiding it. The NY session has the highest range and no drift, which is the textbook profile for a premium-selling overlay rather than a directional one. That is outside this study's scope and needs an options venue, but it is where the structure points.
Section 07 · Risk

What would make this wrong

A finding you cannot falsify is not a finding. These are the specific conditions under which this study's conclusion stops being true.

  • The Asian drift is statistically real but not large. t = 3.00 over 2,914 sessions, positive in 10 of 12 years — and negative in 2018 and 2022. A two-year losing stretch is inside normal behaviour for this edge, which means you cannot distinguish a bad run from a dead edge quickly. Decide the abandonment rule in advance.
  • The sample is one regime. 2015–2026 is a secular gold bull market: $1,184 to $4,572, with 2025 alone up 51.7%. The long-side results, the 200-day filter and the Asia drift may all be partly an expression of that trend. This study cannot separate them, and a genuine bear decade would be the real test.
  • Volatility regime shift. ADR went from $12 to $128 within the sample. ATR-normalisation is the defence, but a fresh doubling would still change fill quality and spread behaviour in ways backtests do not capture.
  • Crowding. The overnight drift is well documented and increasingly traded. If enough capital moves to the Asian window, the compensation for carrying overnight inventory shrinks and the edge goes with it.
  • Costs are the binding constraint, and they can change. The strategy sits 1–2 bp above break-even on a good venue. A widening of the average spread by 0.2 bp ends it. Monitor realised cost as a live risk metric, not a setup parameter.
  • This is a single-instrument study on a single data feed. Nothing here has been validated on a second vendor's ticks.
On the word “manipulate”

Nothing in this report involves influencing the price of gold, and nothing here would work if it tried. The structure described is a liquidity and inventory pattern — dealers being paid to carry risk through illiquid hours — that you are joining, not creating. Attempting to move a market with $200bn+ in daily turnover is not a strategy; it is a criminal offence with a rounding error's worth of price impact. The edge here comes from being on the right side of the clock, in size you can afford, at a cost low enough to keep it.
